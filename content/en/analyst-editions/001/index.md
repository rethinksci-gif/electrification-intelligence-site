## 1. Executive View

- **Clean electricity gained share without displacing fossil output globally.** Validated clean share rose from approximately 39.42% to 40.91% in 2024, but fossil generation increased 1.69%. The Clean Growth Ratio improved from 0.711 to 0.760 and remained below replacement of incremental supply requirements. H1 is not yet demonstrated globally. [EI-E000109, EI-E000118, EI-E000112, EI-E000121, EI-E000120]
- **H3 strengthened most clearly in passenger-car adoption.** The candidate EU comparison rises from 10% electric-car sales in 2020 to 27% in 2025; the validated publisher-reported global 2025 share is 25%. This supports a changing vehicle market, with fleet turnover and actual fossil substitution still unmeasured. [EI-P4-E010, EI-P4-E011, EI-E000814]
- **The industrial electricity-price reference improved; heat-cost parity remains unknown.** EU band-IC prices fell from €194.10/MWh in 2024-H2 to €183.70/MWh in 2025-H2. That is a better electricity input-cost starting point. Comparable gas prices, conversion performance and site costs are missing, leaving H4's economic claim the least substantiated. [EI-E000610, EI-E000612]
- **The battery headline has not established dispatchable solar economics.** The validated $70/kWh stationary-pack benchmark and US commissioned battery cohort are relevant inputs. Neither establishes cheaper matched service than gas or fewer gas price-setting hours. H2 remains uncertain at the system level. [EI-E000822, EI-E000812]
- **Grid access and equipment procurement are the priority bottleneck watch, not a proved ranking.** IEA's validated procurement-pressure claim supports concern. Missing comparable K19/K20 observations prevent identifying the dominant system constraint or showing whether it is worsening. [EI-E000826]
- **The strongest manufacturing implication is to evaluate delivered useful service at the site.** A lower regional electricity reference does not establish that a particular electric process is competitive. Connection availability, operating conditions and useful-output cost can change the investment conclusion; the current data do not quantify their combined effect. **Interpretation**, grounded in the tariff and procurement evidence.
- **The most concrete materials lead is industrial thermal storage.** Rondo's source identifies an operating heat-battery case and brick/wire materials. It supports a component-level investigation of durability and qualification; a premium material opportunity or demand forecast remains speculative. [EI-E000824, EI-E000827]

## 3. Three Signals That Matter Most

### Signal 1 — A cleaner global power mix still accompanied more fossil generation

> [!what-changed] What Changed · Validated
> **What changed — validated:** Clean share increased to 40.91% in 2024; the Clean Growth Ratio rose from 0.711 to 0.760. Fossil generation nevertheless rose 1.69%. The EU moved differently, with fossil output down 8.37%. [EI-E000109, EI-E000118, EI-E000112, EI-E000121, EI-E000120, EI-E000164]

**Why it matters — interpretation:** The distinction between supplying additional electricity and replacing fossil output changes the thesis verdict. The global data support a cleaner mix, while actual displacement is regional.

**KPI / thesis:** K01 clean share, K03 Clean Growth Ratio, K04 fossil generation YoY; H1.

**Industrial consequence — interpretation:** A manufacturer cannot infer fossil-independent electricity supply or a regional competitive advantage from the global clean share. Procurement and siting decisions require the relevant regional system and delivered service.

**Material consequence:** No direct material implication identified yet. These aggregate generation changes do not identify component intensity, substitution or new material specifications.

**Watch next:** Comparable annual supply and fossil-output changes, with trade and measurement boundaries reconciled. Sustained absolute fossil decline would be more decisive than another clean-share headline.

### Signal 2 — EU electric-car adoption crossed a watch threshold, with substitution still unmeasured

> [!what-changed] What Changed · Candidate
> **What changed — candidate:** The selected IEA chart shows EU BEV+PHEV passenger-car sales share rising from 10% in 2020 to 27% in 2025. Both pilot evidence records remain candidates. Separately, the existing validated global 2025 statistic is 25%; it is not independent confirmation of the EU comparison. [EI-P4-E010, EI-P4-E011, EI-E000814]

**Why it matters — interpretation:** The five-year shift is relevant to vehicle production planning. It supports H3's adoption mechanism, but does not establish an annual acceleration, a persistent diffusion breakpoint or a corresponding fall in oil use.

**KPI / thesis:** K11 EV sales share; H3. K12 fleet share and actual electric driving remain gaps.

**Industrial consequence — interpretation:** Product and production planning should distinguish new-car demand from the much slower question of how the operating fleet changes. The dataset does not identify which plants, suppliers or processes gain share.

**Material consequence:** No direct material implication identified yet. Vehicle sales share alone does not establish material demand without vehicle volumes, component choices and material intensity.

**Watch next:** Human verification of the candidate comparison, intervening annual observations, fleet penetration and electric vehicle-kilometres, including PHEV electric use.

### Signal 3 — The EU electricity reference became cheaper, without proving electric-heat parity

> [!what-changed] What Changed · Validated
> **What changed — validated:** The comparable EU non-household band-IC reference fell from €194.10/MWh in 2024-H2 to €183.70/MWh in 2025-H2. This is an absolute end-user price reference, not a cross-region price spread or a wholesale spot price. [EI-E000610, EI-E000612]

**Why it matters — interpretation:** Electricity input cost improved on the recorded basis. The useful-heat comparison remains unresolved because matching gas prices, conversion factors and site costs have not been acquired.

**KPI / thesis:** K15 reference-price variant; relevant to K16 useful-heat cost ratio, which remains unpopulated. H4.

**Industrial consequence — interpretation:** Revisit a process-specific electricity-cost input where the consumption band and tariff basis fit. Do not apply this EU aggregate to a US installation, a PPA or every factory.

**Material consequence:** No direct material implication identified yet. A cheaper tariff does not demonstrate orders for electrical equipment or changes in component specifications.

**Watch next:** Matched factory electricity/gas bills, operating temperatures, measured conversion performance, utilization and connection costs. Only then can a process-level economic threshold be assessed.

## 4. One Signal That Looks Important But Is Probably Noise

### The $70/kWh stationary-pack headline as a claim of gas displacement

**Validated fact:** The BNEF source assigns $70/kWh to its 2025 stationary battery-pack segment. It is not a turnkey BESS cost, a cell price or a verified China-only price. Survey weighting and the full energy/price basis are not established in the public segment description. [EI-E000822]

**The noisy inference:** Treating that price as proof that dispatchable solar now beats gas. The current canonical record supplies no matched prior-year pack observation, installed-system cost, charging profile, delivered duration, reliability obligation or gas-service comparator. This edition therefore makes no measured pack-price-decline claim.

> [!what-did-not-change] What Did Not Change
> The benchmark is useful as an input to a future cost study. Its repetition does not change H2, and Phase 3's revalidation of the same source is not independent confirmation. The dataset is too thin to conclude anything about matched-service parity from this number alone.

## 6. Deep Dive

### Clean Growth Ratio: when a cleaner mix starts replacing fossil output

**The mechanism.** Electricity can become cleaner in percentage terms while fossil generation continues to increase. The distinction depends on how much electricity demand grows and how much of that additional supply comes from clean generation. Clean share describes the composition of a year's output. The Clean Growth Ratio describes the relationship between two annual changes. An analyst needs both, plus absolute fossil output, to distinguish a changing mix from actual displacement.

The project's canonical K03 is:

**Clean Growth Ratio = change in domestic clean generation / change in reported system supply requirement.**

Clean includes renewables and nuclear. The denominator uses reported gross domestic generation plus net imports. It is not final billed electricity consumption or a peak-load measurement. Numerator and denominator must use aligned periods and source conventions. This boundary matters especially when comparing countries: electricity consumed inside a border can come from outside it, while domestic generators can serve external demand.

**What the current calculation says.** The validated global ratio is 0.760 for 2024, compared with 0.711 for 2023. Its stored lineage, EI-L000008, uses clean-generation inputs of 12,654.72 and 11,693.19 TWh, and supply-requirement inputs of 30,930.23 and 29,665.34 TWh. Their annual differences are 961.53 and 1,264.89 TWh respectively. Dividing the former by the latter reproduces the recorded ratio. These are existing source inputs and an existing calculation, not estimates added for this edition. [EI-E000113, EI-E000101, EI-E000117, EI-E000106, EI-E000121, EI-E000112]

**Interpretation:** Clean generation supplied approximately three-quarters of the measured global increase. That is material progress, but it left room for fossil output to grow. The independent arithmetic check within the same dataset is the recorded fossil-generation increase of 1.69%. “Independent” here means a different check on the accounting conclusion, not an independent source. The clean-share increase to 40.91% and continued fossil growth are therefore compatible findings. [EI-E000118, EI-E000120]

**Why the denominator deserves scrutiny.** At national level, define supply requirement as domestic clean generation plus domestic fossil generation plus net imports. With aligned categories, the accounting relationship is:

**Change in fossil generation = change in supply requirement − change in net imports − change in clean generation.**

A domestic clean-growth ratio above one does not, by itself, prove domestic fossil decline if the trade term moves materially. Conversely, falling domestic fossil output can coexist with increased reliance on imports. The useful question is whether the system actually reduced fossil generation on a clearly defined boundary. Neither an imported clean-power assumption nor a consumption-based denominator should be inserted into a generation-based series without rebuilding the measurement contract.

Storage creates another boundary issue. Charging is already a use of supplied electricity and must not be added to demand a second time. Discharge is not new primary generation. The project's source-specific method notes retain residual storage-accounting limitations; they do not pretend these have vanished. Curtailment is electricity that was not generated, so potential curtailed output is not added to the numerator. These rules keep a measured generation ratio separate from a claim about theoretical renewable potential.

**Where interpretation goes wrong.** The first mistake is treating a rising clean share as fossil displacement. The second is treating a single ratio above a selected threshold as proof of permanence. The project's 0.7, 1.0 and 1.2 bands are research heuristics. The original stronger-displacement rule asks for more than 1.2 across two comparable annual periods with fossil decline; the available global ratios do not meet it. Weather, trade, coverage revisions and storage conventions can affect individual comparisons. The project has not calibrated those bands into a probability of structural change.

A third mistake is using the ratio when demand growth is zero, negative or too small relative to input uncertainty. A small denominator can magnify an otherwise ordinary change. In those cases the useful evidence is the absolute change in clean and fossil TWh, together with the reason demand moved. The project withholds nonpositive-denominator ratios and checks input-rounding sensitivity. That check addresses rounding, not the larger uncertainty in coverage or subsequent source revisions.

**What the regional contrast adds.** EU fossil generation fell 8.37% in 2024 while global fossil output rose. This is evidence that displacement can occur in a region without establishing a global turning point. It does not identify the cause of the entire EU decline, nor does it isolate weather, demand, trade or each technology's contribution. The analyst should retain the contrast rather than compress it into one statement that the whole transition either accelerated or failed. [EI-E000164, EI-E000120]

**What remains uncertain.** The accepted power baseline ends in 2024. Explicitly estimated latest-year Ember values were withheld during Phase 3, so this edition cannot claim that the recorded pattern persisted into 2025 or 2026. K03 also says little about hourly adequacy, marginal price-setting or the availability of power at a specific connection. A clean annual energy balance and a dependable industrial electricity service answer different questions. K08/K09's missing service and dispatch observations remain consequential.

**Why manufacturing should care.** For a factory evaluating electrification, this ratio is a system-context indicator, not an investment calculator. A cleaner annual supply mix can justify closer investigation of a region, but it supplies neither the delivered tariff nor the cost of useful heat. The observed EU electricity-price improvement is a separate piece of evidence. It still needs a matching process duty, conversion factor, gas alternative and connection assessment before it can support an investment conclusion. [EI-E000610, EI-E000612]

> [!what-would-change-our-view] What Would Change Our View
> The practical analytical task is to connect three levels without skipping one: measured system substitution, the electricity service available to a site, and useful output from the industrial process. Current evidence is strongest at the first level's annual accounting and weakest at the link to plant performance. The next convincing H1 update should show repeated clean growth covering aligned supply growth and actual fossil decline; the next convincing industrial conclusion must then demonstrate what that system change does to a named manufacturing service.

The validated record supports Rondo's reported 100 MWhth operating thermal-storage case and its stated materials. It validates the attribution to the company, not an independent performance audit. Energy capacity is not delivered thermal power or annual utilization. [EI-E000824, EI-E000827]

**Uncertainty:** No grade, bill of materials, replacement rate, measured degradation or specification comparison is recorded. Volume growth and specification tightening are both unquantified. Existing commodity materials may already meet the duty; that is a risk to any premium-material thesis. This evidence does not support a PA66, specialty-polymer or advanced-ceramic demand forecast.

> [!materials-hypothesis] Materials Hypothesis
> The worthwhile materials question is specific: which component fails or limits performance under the actual thermal duty, and would a different material improve useful lifetime or cost? Until those measurements exist, the opportunity remains **speculative**.

## 9. What I Am Waiting For

1. **Two comparable annual power releases showing clean growth covering supply growth and absolute fossil decline.** Reconcile trade and storage before changing the global H1 view.
2. **An annual operating dataset linking battery charging, peak discharge and gas price-setting, plus a matched solar/storage-versus-gas service comparison.** This would test H2's mechanism rather than its component inputs.
3. **Consecutive EU EV observations above the pilot threshold, independently checked and linked to fleet use and direct-fossil displacement.** This could upgrade the present watch-only classification.
4. **A commercial industrial process with metered useful output, utilization and matched electricity/gas economics, accompanied by component specifications and failure data.** This would test H4 and turn the H6 material chain into an assessable opportunity.
5. **Comparable completed and pending industrial-load connections, plus realized transformer/cable delivery cohorts.** Cause-coded delays and consistent clocks would show whether infrastructure is becoming the dominant constraint or easing.
